PD-01

Pocket Wrestler

Prototype

The crimp wrestler is a tabletop finger-strength duel that climbers love. Pocket Wrestler shrinks it into a single-finger version that clips to your chalk bag.

Two players hook a finger into opposing tracks and pull. The open outer frame doubles as a structural loop, so it takes a standard carabiner and goes wherever your gear goes.

It's built for 3D printing: three parts, no supports, and a snap-fit that holds up to repeated pulls without a single screw or drop of glue. I rendered twelve colorways, each named after a Manila climbing gym.

PD-03

Urban Mobility E‑trike

Concept

Mandaluyong's e-trikes still run on rules borrowed from the gas tricycle. This project redesigns the vehicle and the way its routes operate.

The cabin is planned around passengers, with room to move, secure handholds and proper storage. A wide, sweeping windshield opens up the driver's view of the road.

The faceted shell is shaped for low drag, which improves efficiency and stretches battery range across a full day of trips.

PD-02

E‑kuliglig Tractor

Concept

The kuliglig, the Filipino hand tractor, is the workhorse of small farms. Its design has barely moved: an exposed diesel engine that hits the farmer with fumes, noise and vibration all day.

E-kuliglig swaps diesel for a 15 kW electric drive. A sealed chassis protects the operator and keeps mud and rain out of the internals, which cuts maintenance and adds years to the machine.

Speed and power controls sit in the handlebars, so the operator never has to let go. The rear hitch stays modular for plows, harrows and cultivators.

PD-04

Jolt Board

Concept

A 27-inch electric board for city commuters: light enough to carry all day, stable enough for busy streets.

The deck is made from renewable bamboo and abaca fiber, tough but with some flex. Dual hub motors keep every wire out of sight, and an integrated lithium-ion pack does 15–20 mph for 8–10 miles.

The remote works in either hand, keeps the controls to the essentials, and has a safety strap so it stays with you in traffic.

FW-01

Wave Technical Footwear

3 prototypes + final

The rainy season here lasts for months. Wave started as a design challenge: a high-top slip-on I could wear every day, whatever the forecast.

It became a test of local capability too. I pushed our manufacturing partners beyond traditional leather shoemaking to prove they could build a world-class technical sneaker, and it took four rounds to get there.

Set the high-top silhouette: black neoprene, elastic straps and a water-resistant zip. A white skirt stretches the look of a standard cupsole.

FW-05

PAMBA Sneakers

Prototype

PAMBA is a handmade premium casual sneaker in two colorways, each drawn from a Filipino icon.

The silhouette is a clean, low-profile court shoe with a perforated toe, a padded collar, a serrated-edge cupsole and gold-tipped laces. It's meant to be the everyday pair that goes with everything.

The story lives in the materials. Each colorway layers several leathers and suedes in close tones, so the shoe reads as one color from across the room and shows its texture up close.

FW-06

Patchwork Rag Basketball

Concept

Almost every Filipino home has one: the round patchwork basahan, a rag stitched together from fabric offcuts and sold by street vendors.

This basketball concept borrows its construction. The upper is built from mixed-material panels, each treated like a separate offcut, and joined with heavy contrast stitching that mimics the handmade rag.

The result gives a structured performance shape a fragmented, textured look, and a nod to the everyday resourcefulness behind the original.

Profile

About

Background, toolkit, experience and recognition.

I'm Nicco, a product and footwear designer from Manila. I design products, shoes and the systems around them.

I studied industrial design at the University of Santo Tomas and have spent the years since moving between product, footwear, apparel, exhibition and graphic design, including two years as an art director leading a team of designers. Today I'm Product Design & Innovation Lead at Music Tribe. Footwear has been the constant throughout. I've designed shoes since 2018, and several have gone into factory production.

My process runs from quick sketches to CAD, Blender renders and physical prototypes, whether that's a sample off a factory line or a 3D print on my desk. AI tools like Vizcom and Claude are part of that workflow too. They speed up concept visuals, colorway tests and research, and help me build custom tools that streamline the process.

I'm at my best on products that fit where they're used: built for local weather, local streets and the makers who produce them.
